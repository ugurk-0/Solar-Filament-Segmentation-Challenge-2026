\begin{abstract}
We study instance segmentation of solar filaments in 2048$\times$2048 GONG
H-$\alpha$ images using an attention-gated ResNet-18 U-Net and segmentation-mask
losses. Grouping duplicate annotator records by physical observation avoids
exact-image leakage. On a fixed 85-observation local assessment, mean Panoptic
Quality (PQ) increased from 0.0706 with the original watershed configuration
to 0.2632 after connected-component calibration and guided fine-tuning.
The assessment was reused across rounds; these are exploratory local results,
not a leaderboard score or an isolated ablation of architectural contributions.
\end{abstract}

\section{Task and Data Analysis}
Multiple annotator records can describe the same physical observation.
We group records by the final hyphen-separated filename stem and deduplicate
validation to one annotation set per observation. Losses and evaluation use an
approximate projected $\pm70^{\circ}$ longitude support with fixed disk radius
and zero solar tilt. This does not eliminate temporal correlation between images.

\section{Method}
\subsection{Model}
U-Net over a timm \texttt{features\_only} encoder (ResNet-18 default), additive
attention gates on skip connections~\cite{oktay2018}, and a residual dilated
bottleneck ($d\!=\!1,2,4$; GroupNorm scale zero-initialised so training starts
from the plain-U-Net function). The segmentation head is trained from polygon masks;
other ground-truth metadata is not used as a training target.

\subsection{Loss}
$\mathcal{L} = 0.3\,\mathrm{BCE} + 0.3\,\mathrm{softDice} + 0.4\,(1-\mathrm{clDice})$,
all terms masked to the annotated limb region. Soft-clDice~\cite{shit2021} compares differentiable skeletons and
penalises filament fragmentation invisible to Dice.

\subsection{Training}
Filament-centred 768$\times$768 crops (50\% in Round 5; other crops sampled uniformly), dihedral augmentations
(flips/rot90 are physically valid), photometric jitter, AMP, AdamW +
cosine schedule, weight EMA.

\subsection{Inference and Post-processing}
Overlap-add sliding windows use size 1024 px and overlap 256 px.
The selected model is a single fold-1 EMA checkpoint (Round 5 epoch 3).
Connected components use threshold 0.65 and minimum area 200, selected on
40 calibration observations. Local assessments use no test-time augmentation
(TTA); the CSV uses eight-way dihedral TTA. The exact exported inference
configuration has not been assessed locally with the reported protocol.

\section{Experiments}
Fold 1 contains 141 deduplicated validation observations: 16 for epoch
monitoring, 40 for converter calibration, and 85 for assessment. PQ uses
greedy one-to-one matching at IoU $\geq0.5$; mean observation PQ and pooled
dataset PQ are reported separately.
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrr@{}}
\toprule
Configuration & Mean PQ & Dataset PQ \\
\midrule
Original, watershed & 0.0706 & 0.0706 \\
Original, calibrated components & 0.2073 & 0.2177 \\
Round 1, calibrated components & 0.2454 & 0.2511 \\
Round 5, calibrated components & 0.2632 & 0.2626 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
Rounds 2--4 did not exceed the calibrated starting monitor score and were
rejected. Round 5 reduced the BCE positive-weight cap to 1.0 and the probability
of a filament-centred crop to 0.5. Its epoch monitor PQ values were 0.3060,
0.2871, and 0.3102. On the assessment subset, it produced 305 true positives,
536 false positives, and 343 false negatives.

Relative to the original calibrated checkpoint, the paired mean-PQ gain was
0.0559 (observation-bootstrap 95\% interval [0.0325, 0.0812]). This interval is
conditional on one split, seed, and selected checkpoint. The assessment was
reused across rounds and is not an untouched final test. No controlled
architecture/loss ablation, multi-fold confirmation, or leaderboard result is
claimed. The generated CSV contains 1,837 instance masks over 180 processed
test images; all saved masks passed COCO RLE validation.

\section{Conclusion}
We hypothesise that skeleton-aware losses and instance-aware post-processing
improve PQ on thin solar structures. Controlled ablations are still required
to measure their contributions and compare them with architecture changes.
